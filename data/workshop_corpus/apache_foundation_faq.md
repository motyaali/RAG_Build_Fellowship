# Apache Software Foundation — frequently asked questions

A short, illustrative FAQ drawn from public information about the Apache
Software Foundation. Used here as a sample document for the RAG demo; for
authoritative answers consult <https://www.apache.org>.

## What is the Apache Software Foundation?

The Apache Software Foundation (ASF) is an American non-profit corporation
incorporated in Delaware in 1999. It exists to support a community of
open-source software projects, all of which are developed under a common
governance model and released under the Apache License, version 2.0.

## How many projects does the ASF host?

The ASF is the home of more than 350 top-level projects as of the mid-2020s,
covering web servers (httpd), big data (Hadoop, Spark, Kafka), build tooling
(Maven, Ant), search (Lucene, Solr), and many other categories. New projects
typically enter through the Apache Incubator before graduating to top-level
status.

## What is the Apache License?

The Apache License 2.0 is a permissive open-source licence. Anyone may use,
modify, and redistribute Apache-licensed software for any purpose, including
commercial use, provided they preserve the copyright and licence notices and
note any significant changes they have made. It also includes an explicit
grant of patent rights from contributors to users.

## How is the ASF governed?

The ASF is run by an elected Board of Directors. Each project is led by a
Project Management Committee (PMC), composed of committers who have earned
that role through sustained contributions. The PMC has final authority over
the project's technical direction, releases, and membership. The "Apache
Way" emphasises consensus, openness, and community over code.

## Who pays for the ASF?

The ASF is funded by tax-deductible donations from individuals, by corporate
sponsorships, and by event revenue (notably ApacheCon). It has no paid
engineering staff — all project development is done by volunteers, although
many of those volunteers are paid by their employers to work on ASF projects.

## How do I contribute to an Apache project?

Each project sets its own contribution process, but the common pattern is:

1. Read the project's `CONTRIBUTING.md` and developer mailing list archives.
2. Pick a small, well-scoped issue from the project's bug tracker.
3. Submit a patch (typically via a GitHub pull request or a JIRA-attached
   patch file, depending on the project's age).
4. Iterate on review feedback in public, on the project mailing list.
5. After sustained contributions, an existing committer may nominate you for
   commit access. PMC membership follows from there.

## How does the ASF differ from the Linux Foundation?

Both are non-profit open-source umbrella organisations, but their models
differ. The ASF hosts projects under a single licence and a single,
community-driven governance model. The Linux Foundation operates more as a
trade association, hosting projects with varied licences and structures, and
employs engineering staff directly on flagship efforts such as the Linux
kernel and Kubernetes.
